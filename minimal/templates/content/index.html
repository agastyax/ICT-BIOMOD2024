The increasing concentration of atmospheric carbon dioxide is a major driver of climate change, creating an urgent need for effective carbon capture, utilization, and storage (CCUS) technologies. CCUS involves capturing carbon dioxide from industrial emissions and either converting or storing it to mitigate its environmental impact.
Carbonic anhydrase (CA), an enzyme that catalyzes the rapid conversion of carbon dioxide into bicarbonate ions, plays a key role in enhancing the efficiency of this process. Procuring this CA enzyme is discussed in the following two points
Using genetic engineering, we established a protocol where the CA gene from Saccharomyces cerevisiae was expressed in Pichia pastoris
this was achieved with the help of Escherichia coli, a widely used host for recombinant protein production due to its ability to ensure proper protein folding, its capacity to grow to high cell densities, and its secretion of recombinant proteins into the external environment.
Simultaneously, we synthesized ferromagnetic nanoparticles and functionalized it with a carboxyl group enhancing its stability, reusability, and separation efficiency. CA from spinach was immobilized and their activity was measured using different quantitative techniques. We aim to further research and study the activity of different CA enzymes immobilized on the nanoparticles.
This immobilized CA system offers a sustainable approach to capture carbon dioxide and convert it into cellular biomass. The biomass can then be processed to produce valuable products, such as biofuels, antioxidants, biogas, etc. In conclusion, this research contributes to carbon recycling and environmental sustainability.
