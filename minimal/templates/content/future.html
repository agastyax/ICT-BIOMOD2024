FUTURE

Many CA enzymes have been identified and characterized in recent years (Faridi & Satyanarayana, 2016; Hou et al., 2019; Li et al., 2015) However, proper kinetic data for these enzymes, when immobilized on magnetite nanoparticles, is lacking. The first step was to establish a set of procedures for producing enzymes immobilized on cMNPs, which the team has successfully accomplished. With these established protocols, we aim to produce different origin CA enzymes through Pichia pastoris and study their activity once immobilized on our cMNPs. Upon optimization of the process, we plan to implement these nanoparticles in biomass production (Yuan et al., 2022). Why biomass? With depleting fossil fuel reserves, the search for alternative energy sources is ongoing. The thermal treatment of biomass through techniques like gasification, hydrotreatment, and pyrolysis generates bio-oil, biofuels, bio-char, and biogas, which can help address the energy crisis (Kumar et al., 2020). Using CA-functionalized magnetite nanoparticles can help overcome the mass transfer resistance due to the low solubility of CO2, simplifying downstream processing. The conversion of CO2 to H2CO3 in the presence of water increases overall solubility, enhancing the growth rate of photosynthetic microbes for biomass production. Another interesting question: how can we integrate these nanoparticles into the reactor system? This is a question we may address in the future!
